Nine Doors

Visual concepts for the nine provisional entry doors, drawn to discuss a visual language.

Not production assets

These are drawings made to argue about treatment, not artwork for the app. The creative direction is pre-produced hybrid live action and generated imagery, and the governing plan is explicit that this "does not authorize asset generation here." Scenario situations and door titles are unreviewed drafts. Nothing here is approved content.

The deck

One plate per door. Each shows a situation rather than a subject or a mood — a thing happening that a person could step into. The nine share one palette and one line weight so the browse reads as a set, and none depicts a face.

What the drawings are arguing

Four decisions sit behind the deck. Each is contestable, and each is the kind of thing worth settling before any real asset is produced.

Situations, not subjects

Each plate shows something happening that a person could step into, rather than an object or a mood. A picture of a beehive is a subject; a hive standing where it was promised not to stand is a situation.

This follows SA01 directly: entrances are situational, never categories.

No faces

Nobody is depicted. A face invites the viewer to read age, gender, class or ethnicity into the scenario, which is the identity inference FR02 forbids the app from making.

Absence of people also keeps the situation available: the viewer can occupy it rather than watch someone else in it.

One palette, one line

All nine share a muted palette and a single line weight, so a swipe through the deck reads as one set of related invitations rather than nine competing posters.

Warm accent marks the one element carrying the tension — the near hive, the second stake, the worn route.

The tension is drawn, not captioned

Where possible the difficulty is visible in the composition: a measured gap, a stopped line, an empty outline where a source should be, a dotted route nobody takes.

This matters for accessibility. If a plate needs its caption to make sense, the text alternative cannot carry the core content as required.

What to avoid

  • Faces, bodies, or anything from which a viewer can read an identity into the scenario.
  • Contemporary political or religious markers — flags, insignia, recognisable settings. The scenario should not arrive already taking a side.
  • Picturesque cultural detail used as decoration. The spec warns against presenting generic cultural experiences, and an unfamiliar setting that is merely ornamental fails that test.
  • Visual hierarchy across the nine. If one plate is more arresting than the rest, the deck has ranked the doors, and the shuffle intended to keep them equal cannot undo it.
  • Any image implying an outcome — a resolved handshake, a clear sky after storm. That is AT17 territory in pictures.
  • Imagery that only resolves in motion. These need to work as the still frame a reel opens on.

Open questions for the content owner

  • Is the displaced setting right, or does it read as evasive to someone in live difficulty? Worth testing two contemporary plates against two displaced ones.
  • Should the plates be drawn, photographed, or hybrid? The creative direction says hybrid live action and generated imagery; drawings may undercut that, or may suit the no-faces constraint better than photography can.
  • Does every door need a plate, or do the two low-stakes doors — "Just curious" and "Self-understanding" — want a quieter treatment that signals a shorter path?
