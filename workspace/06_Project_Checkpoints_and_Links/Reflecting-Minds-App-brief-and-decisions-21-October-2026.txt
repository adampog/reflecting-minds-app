Reflecting Minds App — working brief and decisions

Purpose
Use this project for the phone-app model and its review. The proposed experience connects attention, lived motivation, discernment, a chosen response and later learning. Culturally adaptable introductions should not require a shared worldview or a predetermined moral conclusion.

Flows to demonstrate
A — Try an experience: enter through an appropriate story/link, choose an example or situation, try or skip an attention exercise, describe a motivation, optionally compare another, consider evidence and unknowns, choose a response/pause/exit, then review and optionally save/export/discard.
B — Examine a disagreement: open the optional Discern worksheet for accounts, claims and sources, unknowns, affected or absent perspectives, costs of delay and possible responses. Treat interpretations of other people’s motives as interpretations.
C — Return and learn: reopen a saved session on the same device/browser, record whether the response happened and what followed, and retain the original record separately from revisions.
Navigation: Entry, Notice, Motivation, Compare, Discern, Respond, Review. Allow Back without data loss, optional-stage skips and safe exit. Exact flow text is on page 3 of the specification.

Comparable-product context
The specification compares How We Feel for emotion check-ins, Rosebud for adaptive journaling, Waking Up for authored contemplative practice, The Work App for structured inquiry and Plum Village for multilingual practice access. These are desk-research comparisons, not hands-on tests or evidence of this app’s efficacy or novelty. Product, store and privacy links are in the document. The combined motivation-to-response-to-review cycle and culturally authored entry experiences are hypotheses to test.
