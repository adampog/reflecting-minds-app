## Human turns (verbatim)

> In order to make this project successful, the only motivation people need is the motivation
> to reduce emotional friction, internally and interpersonally. We don't have to motivate them
> to do that, we just need to find the way in, together.

## Assistant turns (verbatim) — commentary, not direction

> That framing puts the work in the right place: you're not building motivation from scratch,
> you're finding the door that's already there. People generally don't need convincing that
> friction hurts. What they need is a way in that doesn't feel like being asked to change,
> admit fault, or do emotional labor on someone else's schedule.
>
> One nuance to keep in view: the desire to reduce friction is nearly universal, but it can
> pull in two directions. Avoiding a hard conversation reduces friction right now, while
> having it reduces friction over time. So the "finding the way in together" part may be less
> about motivating people and more about making the durable route feel as safe and cheap as
> the avoidant one. Small, concrete, low-stakes starting points tend to do that well,
> especially ones where people name the friction themselves rather than having it named for
> them.
>
> Doing it together also matters in its own right, because co-creating the entry point is
> itself a low-friction experience, which builds trust in the process.
>
> What's the project, and who are the people you're hoping to bring in? That would help me
> think about what a good way in might look like for them.

> Great, I'm glad to have Alan's technical perspective in the mix. I'm ready to take in
> whatever he shares.
>
> One thing worth keeping in mind as the technical details come in: they'll likely shape what
> the "way in" can realistically look like. Some constraints may close off certain approaches,
> while others may open doors you haven't considered yet. So as Alan lays things out, it could
> help to listen for where the friction already lives in the system or workflow, such as
> handoffs, ambiguous ownership, or places where people have to guess at each other's
> intentions. Those are often the most natural entry points, since the friction is already
> visible and nobody has to be asked to surface it.
>
> Feel free to paste in what Alan shares, or have him describe it directly, and we can work
> out together how it connects to the people side of the project.

## Why this is filed here

It bears on an open gap: the bundle never defines the audience. This excerpt answers that
question — the audience is a state rather than a demographic — which is consistent with FR02's
prohibition on inferring identity and with SA01's situational entrances.

Analysis of what it changes is in the v0.2 review, under "The friction framing."

One caution carried forward: the assistant's suggestion of **co-creating the entry point with
participants** would need checking against FR15 (no in-app feedback prompts) and the
non-formal-pilot boundary before it became a beta activity. Co-creation before the beta, with
people who are not participants, raises no such question.
