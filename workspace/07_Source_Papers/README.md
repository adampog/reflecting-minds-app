## Davis, J. H. (2023) — *Theravāda Buddhist philosophy*

Routledge Encyclopedia of Philosophy · 12 pages
SHA256 `1f1bb1fc9e15602264dc45536ad5990bab23ec3b945fe85ec894f9243b5568e0`

**Supplied by** Jake H. Davis, in `#all-reflecting-minds`, 23 October 2026, as the grounding
for the friction framing. The author is the project participant.

### Why it is in the project

Asked whether the framing — that the only motivation people need is the motivation to reduce
emotional friction, and that the work is finding the way in rather than creating the motivation
— was his, Jake confirmed it, and placed it:

> It is mine. (...based on my reading of dukkha as 'un-ease' - I'll include a link below) But I
> would still keep it as a suggestion rather than a direction, as I am open to iterating better
> ways of framing this.

So the framing is attributed, and its **status is deliberately a suggestion, not direction**.
That distinction is his and should be preserved wherever the framing is cited.

### The concept it grounds

The paper treats *dukkha* as central to Buddhist thought and as the opposite of *sukha*, ease
— rendered in the text as "dis-ease." It is presented as something to be thoroughly understood
rather than merely endured, with a cause that can be abandoned and a cessation that can be
realised. The paper covers considerably more than this; the above is only the thread the
product's framing draws on.

This is what "emotional friction" in the project's framing is pointing at. Worth noting for
anyone writing scenario or product copy: the source concept is broader and less cheerful than
"friction" suggests, and it is a tradition's technical term rather than a metaphor. The
framing borrows the shape of the idea, not its full content.

### Constraints on use

- The paper is a published encyclopedia article under its publisher's rights. It is held here
  as reference for project participants. Do not reproduce it, in whole or in part, in product
  copy, scenarios, marketing or any published artefact.
- The product makes no claim to teach, represent or apply Buddhist practice. AT17 forbids
  claiming the app produces beneficial outcomes, and a tradition's authority is not a route
  around that.
- Scenario content must stay free of religious markers, per specification §6. Grounding a
  design concept in this source does not license religious framing in the experience.
