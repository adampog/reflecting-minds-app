## (a) What Jake asked for directly
1. Around nine entry experiences, with an intuitive, seamless, randomised way to choose among them, for example swiping through short reels to see which one grabs you.
2. The first beta does not need to be a formal research pilot (settled). It would help to learn not only whether the app is understandable and usable, but whether participants feel they got something out of it, ideally through a behavioural sign rather than an explicit question.
3. From the beginning, the architecture should allow for:
   - future use in larger group conflicts, where groups hold very different conceptual and value frameworks and may not share a language, so different linguistic cultures and sub-cultures must be supported (the three-party land-dispute example illustrates this);
   - keeping Global Attentiveness as a guiding question while staying open to other changes in attention and motivation mattering more;
   - users and groups suggesting their own strategies, in a non-partisan spirit of acting "as if".
   (The message announces four things but lists three; no fourth has been assumed.)
4. Possibly letting initial beta invitees invite others and form teams or groups.
5. Beta choices Jake submitted for Alan and Jake to review: a progressive web app, saving on the device, opt-in research, AI later, invited testers. These match the defaults ChatGPT had suggested; they are proposals, not decisions.

## What the update supersedes or refines
- The earlier brief asked whether the first beta should be usability-only or a formal study. Jake settled this: not a formal research pilot. It is no longer open.
- The earlier brief deferred groups. Jake's later, tentative question about invitations and teams reopens them conditionally, as a staged proposal.
- Three entry packs become roughly nine entry experiences (Jake's own note in the same message that still listed three).
- The layers are refined: the earlier model put the shared practice in the stable core; the update makes the stable layer principles and safeguards, so the theory of what helps can evolve.
- Optional AI in the original specification becomes "AI later" for the first beta.

## (b) ChatGPT recommendations and generated-document proposals (not decisions)
- A finite, swipeable card deck with "Surprise me", "Browse all" and a neutral introduction; card order shuffled between visits; presentation order kept separate from experimental assignment.
- Value signals: voluntary saving, a self-chosen response category, voluntary return, and a participant-chosen revision, described as indirect signals and not proof of benefit; never time spent or session length.
- End-of-session choices: keep this reflection, explore further, pause here, finish for now.
- A three-layer model: stable safeguards, adaptable cultural and experiential modules, learning and shared inquiry.
- Conflict-ready data ideas: parties, several perspectives within a party, claims, concepts with original wording and translation candidates, relationships, strategy trials, outcomes.
- A versioned hypothesis registry with Global Attentiveness as one central hypothesis among others.
- A Strategy Library: private, shared with a group, or submitted; attribution named, pseudonymous or anonymous.
- Staged invitations: capped, revocable, expiring links; private groups; invitation is not group membership, and membership is not permission to see private reflections; separate permissions for joining, viewing, contributing strategies, evaluation and research data.
- No AI facilitation, public community or live mediation in the first beta.

## (c) Temporary prototype assumptions (this mockup only)
- The nine entry titles are invented, unreviewed prototype examples. Real titles do not exist yet.
- The multilingual, three-party scenario and every name, term and answer in it are fictional.
- Group, invitation, permission and strategy screens are pictures of proposals, not working features.
- The mockup is a silent captioned walkthrough plus a tap-through viewer of the same screens.

## (d) Still undecided
- Invitation caps (number, time or both); who may create groups.
- What group content, if any, is stored on a server versus shared directly; what moderation the team can provide.
- First-beta languages and locales, and who reviews culturally sensitive translations.
- Which outcome dimensions beyond Global Attentiveness the first evaluation includes.
- Whether strategy suggestions stay local or can be submitted to the team.
- What review is needed before working with real-world conflicts.

## Two tensions this mockup shows but does not resolve
1. **Private, no-account reflection versus real group identity and sharing.** The proposed beta keeps reflection on the device with no account, while groups need some stable identity, membership and shared content. How both can hold is open.
2. **AI later versus the earlier voice demonstration.** The proposals defer AI from the first beta. An earlier mockup showed a scripted, simulated voice conversation with an AI. That video is a separate proposed interaction option; it is not evidence that AI belongs in the first beta.
